1PROBLEM

One slot spoke to everyone — and every surface assumed a creator who already has data.

Campaigns are how TikTok Shop creators find new earning opportunities. The creator home had a single promotional slot, and it showed the same message to a brand-new creator in Indonesia and an established live host in the US.

The surfaces built around that slot carried the same assumption: a creator with sales history, a scheduled LIVE, and a full year of content. In reality the data is uneven — no LIVE this week, zero sign-ups, a first year with no sales, a translation that was never written.

2UX JUDGMENT

Three rules I applied, across three surfaces.

01 · RELEVANCE

Relevance beats volume.

More banners would not help. Targeting by segment and region decides what a creator sees first, so the slot does not have to say everything at once.

Applied in · BFCM creator venue ↓
02 · TRUTHFUL STATE

Show only what is true.

No LIVE scheduled means no card; zero sign-ups means no avatar row reading “0 registered”. A surface that can’t be true should not be drawn.

Applied in · Go-LIVE reminder card ↓
03 · DEGRADATION

Skip, rewrite, or explain.

A year without posts skips that page. A creator with no LIVE gets encouragement, not “0 hours”. A dead end always gets a reason and one next step.

Applied in · Unbox Your Year ↓

3EXPLORATION

Three ways to use the same slot.

I compared options against what creators need on this screen: a quick read of what matters today, without pushing their own sales data below the fold.

A · One static banner

  • Simplest to build and localize
  • Same message for every creator
  • Nothing to learn from beyond one number
Chosen

B · Targeted carousel

  • Slides chosen per segment and region
  • Visible pagination signals more
  • Tap tracking per slide shows what works
  • Needs strict fallback rules

C · Stacked campaign cards

  • Everything visible at once
  • Pushes creators’ own data far down
  • Long translations multiply the height

I worked with product and design to align on B: it keeps the page height fixed, lets targeting do the prioritizing, and turns the slot into something we can measure and iterate on.

EDGE STATES WE HAD TO HANDLE

Without a fallback, a missing translation showed a raw content key to creators. Long Thai and Vietnamese titles wrapped to three lines and covered the CTA.

HOW WE HANDLED THEM

Defined a fallback order for every field, capped titles at two lines with a full-text detail page, and kept CTAs to one line with truncation rules agreed with design. We didn’t shrink the type: smaller text would hurt readability and break the hierarchy across markets, while a fixed two-line limit kept the layout scannable within the established type scale.

6EDGE STATES

Two more surfaces, designed for the data creators actually have.

Real creator data is uneven: no LIVE scheduled, zero sign-ups, a first year with no sales. Each covered state had an explicit rule, preventing blank cards and misleading promises in the implemented flows.

Go-LIVE reminder card

A card above the campaign banners that reminds creators of their next scheduled LIVE.

  • Not yet, but visible. More than a day out, the button reads “Prepare” and stays disabled, so the card informs without nagging.
  • Act now. Within a day, it turns into a red “Go LIVE” button.
  • Hide what isn’t true yet. With zero sign-ups, the avatars and count disappear instead of showing “0 registered”.
  • Fail quietly. No session, or an error, means no card at all, and the rest of the page still renders.

Unbox Your Year

A year-in-review story for creators. Tap “Unbox it!”, then swipe up through the pages.

  • Skip pages with nothing to say. No posts means no “top videos” page. Fewer than four customer states means no map.
  • Rewrite, don’t zero out. A creator with no LIVE gets encouragement, not “0 hours”.
  • Grammar follows the data. One product reads “Your best seller”; three read “Your best sellers”.
  • Every dead end has a way out. Ineligible creators and outdated apps get a clear explanation and one next step.

How it scaled across regions

Let configuration carry regional differences. Lifecycle stages, content, routing, and creator segments moved into validated configuration with fallbacks and feature flags, so a new market became a configuration change rather than a new build.

Agree on the data before the pixels. I ran field and naming reviews with product and backend so components received data in the shape the design assumed — the biggest source of cross-region bugs.

9REFLECTION

What I would do next.

Click-through tells us a slide was noticed, not that it helped. Next I would pair it with campaign join and completion rates, and run short usability sessions with creators in two or three markets to hear how they decide which campaign is worth their time.
